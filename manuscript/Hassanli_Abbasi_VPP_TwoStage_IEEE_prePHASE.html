A Degradation-Aware Two-Stage Framework for Secure VPP Bidding and Feeder-Constrained Dispatch

Amir Hassanli and Ali Reza Abbasi

IEEE manuscript. Option B: provable two-stage LPs. Rows B3, P2 and Full of Table III are not evaluated. Seed 20260827. SMARD DE-LU 6–12 April 2026. Every euro is HiGHS kOptimal, LP gap 0.

Abstract— This paper reports a two-stage LP on a battery-integrated VPP, not a trained LLM–MARL–GNN stack. Stage 1 is a day-ahead energy bid; Stage 2 is a LinDistFlow P–Q correction on the IEEE 33-bus feeder. Sign convention: \(p^{\mathrm{ch}}\ge 0\) charge, \(p^{\mathrm{dis}}\ge 0\) discharge, \(p^{\mathrm{DA}}>0\) export. Negative profit is feeder-service cost (load exceeds local PV). On SMARD DE-LU, 6–12 April 2026, 42/42 programs are HiGHS kOptimal (gap 0). A six-row ablation shows that CVaR does not move expected profit (B1−B0 = +0.24 EUR/day). Throughput aging does (B2−B1 = +90 EUR/day; aging bill 169→69 EUR/day). Voltage safety is a real-time row only: B2 and P1 share \(p^{\mathrm{DA}}\) on every day, and the slack zeros \(276\pm 50\) bus-hours/day below 0.93 pu at 4.3 EUR/day (0.08% of |profit|; Wilcoxon \(p=0.016\), \(n=7\)). P-only cannot hold 0.93 pu; only P–Q holds 0.95 pu (+52 EUR on 8 April). Newton–Raphson mean \(|\Delta V|=0.17\%\), \(\varepsilon_v=0.007\) pu. A physics residual screen sees voltage tapes (stealth recall 0.90) and misses bid/price attacks (−226 / −1565 EUR). Xu daily-range cost saturates at the SoC box and does not discriminate. Rows B3, P2 and Full are empty.

Index Terms— Virtual power plant, CVaR, battery degradation, LinDistFlow, two-stage stochastic programming, voltage constraint, residual FDIA.

I. Introduction

A virtual power plant (VPP) must bid in the day-ahead market and then track that bid on a voltage-constrained feeder while paying a real cell-aging cost [1]–[7]. Four extra blocks—an LLM risk head, distributionally robust reweighting, a multi-agent policy, and a neural FDIA detector—are often stacked on that core. This manuscript does not stack them numerically. It is Option B: a provable two-stage LP with degradation in both stages, CVaR, LinDistFlow, P–Q voltage correction, residual attack tapes, and a written architecture for the rest.

Table III is the ablation. B0, B1, B2 and P1 have HiGHS euros. B3, P2 and Full do not. Section IV writes an LLM tuple, DRO, rainflow/SOH and a QP layer as a protocol. Section V reports only solved programs. Section VI states which claims the week actually supports.

Supported contribution (Table VIII). Throughput aging in the optimiser, and a real-time P–Q LinDistFlow slack that zeros 0.93 pu violations without changing the bid. Not supported on this week: CVaR as an economic lever; Xu as the discriminating aging model; voltage-aware first-stage bidding; cyber-resilience as a headline; LLM/MARL/GNN.

Singh et al. detect VPP FDIA with an autoencoder [15]. This paper’s evaluated occupancy is bidding, aging and voltage, plus a residual (not neural) screen.

IV. Model Redesign (Architecture)

This section is the proposed model. Only the LP specialisation in IV-F is solved.

A. LLM as a Distributional Tuple, Not a Scalar

A pre-gate text map should return four numbers, not one score,

(rt, Δλt, ρt, ht) = fLLM(newst, policyt, reportt), (2)

with rt∈[0,1] a risk index, Δλt a directed price shift, ρt an extra dispersion, and ht a persistence horizon. Scenarios are then

λt,ωadj = λt,ω + rt Δλt + √(1+ρt) εt,ω,   t′≤t+ht. (3)

The head is to be calibrated (Platt, isotonic, conformal) against BERT/FinBERT/XGBoost on Brier, ECE, AUROC, F1 and tail error, then trained decision-focused against trading regret. Not fitted here.

B. DRO Instead of Ad-Hoc Reweighting

Replacing \(\tilde\pi_\omega\propto\pi_\omega(1+\gamma r_t)\) by an ambiguity set avoids a hand-tuned tilt:

maxx minℙ∈𝒰(r) Eℙ[Π(x,ξ)] − β CVaRα(−Π), (4)

𝒰(r) = { ℙ : Df(ℙ,ℙ0) ≤ ε0 + ε1 r }. (5)

Wasserstein \(D_f\) is the intended case. The evaluated Stage 1 is mean–CVaR with uniform \(\pi_\omega=1/K\), rt≡0.

C. Throughput, Xu, Rainflow, SOH

Three aging layers: linear throughput (baseline, evaluated), Xu PWL on daily SoC range (evaluated in the DA objective), rainflow on the realised SoC (post-hoc in Section V). A dynamic SOH,

SOHt+1 = SOHt − a1 DoDta2 − a3 |Pt|,   Pi,tmax = SOHi,t Pirated, (6)

is written and not integrated in the LP. A shadow price \(c_{i,t}^{\mathrm{deg}}=\partial C_i^{\mathrm{deg}}/\partial P_{i,t}\) would mark hours where aging kills arbitrage; Fig. 14 is the evaluated proxy (service cost versus equivalent full cycles from throughput).

D. QP Safety Layer and \(\varepsilon_v\)

If a policy ever emits \(u^{\mathrm{RL}}\), the projection is

usafe = arg minu ‖u − uRL‖W2 (7)

s.t. \(v_{\min}+\varepsilon_v \le v_0 + J_v u \le v_{\max}-\varepsilon_v\), \(|S_\ell|\le S_\ell^{\max}\), SoC boxes. \(\varepsilon_v=0.007\) pu is taken from the Newton–Raphson max error on P1 (Table VI), not from a claim that projection makes violations arbitrarily small. The evaluated Stage 2 is the LP itself, which is already the projection onto LinDistFlow. MARL is not trained. Ramp \(|P_{t}-P_{t-1}|\le \overline r\) was added to the LP; 0.05–0.20 MW/h is non-binding on P1.

E. FDIA

Tapes: \(x_\tau^{\mathrm{att}}=x_\tau+a_\tau^{(k)}\), stealth \(\|H a_\tau\|_2\le\epsilon\). Detectors: residual versus the LinDistFlow twin, and z-score. Autoencoder, Isolation Forest, LSTM and an attacker–defender game are named and not trained. Voltage-channel results are Table VII. Bid/price attacks are economic, not voltage-detectable.

F. Evaluated LPs — Complete Constraints (src/vpp_two_stage.py)

Let \(E^{\Sigma}=\sum_b E^b_b=2.40\) MWh and \(P^{\Sigma}=\sum_b P^b_b=1.20\) MW. Stage 1 is an aggregate battery. Imbalance prices follow (1) with clips \(\lambda^{+}\ge\lambda^{\mathrm{DA}}+5\), \(\lambda^{-}\le\lambda^{\mathrm{DA}}-3\).

Stage-1 objective (B1, B2, P1; B0 sets \(\beta=0\) and drops aging rows):

max   (1−β) Σω πω Πω + β [ η − (1−α)−1 Σω πω zω ] (8)

Πω = Σt [ λDAt,ω pDAt + λ−t,ω δ−t,ω − λ+t,ω δ+t,ω − cdeg(pcht,ω+pdist,ω) ] − Σm κm um,ω. (9)

zω ≥ η − Πω,   zω ≥ 0,   −4 ≤ pDAt ≤ 4. (10)

pDAt + pcht,ω + δ−t,ω − pdist,ω − δ+t,ω = PPVt,ω − 3.715 ℓt,ω, (11)

Et,ω = Et−1,ω + ηc pcht,ω − pdist,ω/ηd,   E0,ω=E24,ω=0.5 EΣ, (12)

0.10 EΣ ≤ Et,ω ≤ 0.90 EΣ,   0 ≤ pcht,ω, pdist,ω ≤ PΣ,   0 ≤ δ±t,ω ≤ 8. (13)

Eminω ≤ Et,ω ≤ Emaxω,   Σm um,ω = (Emaxω−Eminω)/EΣ,   0 ≤ um,ω ≤ wm. (14)

Stage-2 objective (P1; drop ρs in B0/B1/B2):

min Σt [ λ+t δ+t − λ−t δ−t + cdeg Σb (pchb,t+pdisb,t) + ρs Σn sn,t ]. (15)

Eb,t = Eb,t−1 + ηc pchb,t − pdisb,t/ηd,   Eb,0=Eb,24=0.5 Ebb, (16)

0.10 Ebb ≤ Eb,t ≤ 0.90 Ebb,   0 ≤ pchb,t, pdisb,t, q±b,t ≤ Pbb, (17)

pchb,t+pdisb,t+q+b,t+q−b,t ≤ 1.25 Pbb. (18)

Pℓ,t − Σk∈C(j) Pk,t = PL,pkj ℓt − PPVj,t − (pdis−pch)|_{b\to j}, (19)

Qℓ,t − Σk∈C(j) Qk,t = QL,pkj ℓt − (q+−q−)|_{b\to j}, (20)

v21,t=1,   v2j,t = v2i,t − 2(rℓ Pℓ,t+xℓ Qℓ,t)/Sb,   (v̲box)2 ≤ v2n,t ≤ (v̄box)2, (21)

−P1→2,t + δ+t − δ−t = pDAt,   0 ≤ δ±t ≤ 8. (22)

v2n,t + sn,t ≥ (v̲op)2,   sn,t ≥ 0.   (P1, P0) (23)

The box in (21) is a wide numerical bound (0.80–1.15 pu) so LinDistFlow stays defined. The operational floor is (23) at v̲op=0.93 pu (0.95 pu in Table V). Reported profit is (15) without ρs. For P1 and P0 the optimum has s=0, so reported profit equals the complete Stage-2 objective. For rows that still violate (P-only in Table V) the optimiser saw 2500 s but the table omits it; those rows are a theoretical ablation, not an operating policy. B0/B1 omit cdeg in the optimiser and still report 12×throughput. Solver: scipy linprog method="highs" [20], gap 0.

D. Throughput versus Xu

Xu daily-range cost is 85.8 EUR for every model on 8 April because all units hit the 10–90% box. Throughput aging discriminates: B0 2.98 EFC versus P1 1.54 EFC of the 2.4 MWh fleet. Fig. 10. Ramps 0.05–0.20 MW/h do not bind on P1.

The claim that CVaR is economically null is for the operating weight β=0.05 (week B1−B0 = +0.24 EUR/day). A day-8 sweep β ∈ {0, 0.05, 0.15, 0.30, 0.50} moves P1 profit from −6105 to −6182 EUR and pDA from −63.4 to −67.8 MWh. CVaR can bite if β is large; it does not bite at the β of Table III.

VI. Discussion: Claims versus Evidence

Table VIII is the audit of the Option-B claims against the HiGHS week. Two results survive. (i) Throughput aging in the optimiser, not CVaR: B1−B0 is +0.24 EUR/day; B2−B1 is +90 EUR/day (1.7% of |profit|). Xu daily-range cost is 85.8 EUR for every model on 8 April because SoC hits the 10–90% box, so Xu does not discriminate. (ii) Real-time P–Q voltage slack: B2 and P1 share \(p^{\mathrm{DA}}\) on all seven days, so voltage does not enter the bid; it zeros \(276\pm 50\) bus-hours/day at 4.3 EUR/day (0.08% of |profit|; Wilcoxon \(p=0.016\)). P-only cannot hold 0.93 pu; only P–Q holds 0.95 pu. What does not survive: CVaR as an economic innovation; “connect bidding to control” as a first-stage effect; Xu as the aging mechanism that moves euros; cyber-resilience as a headline (bid/price attacks are invisible to the voltage residual; the screen is an upper bound against the same twin). B3/P2/Full remain empty. Day-to-day std of profit is 2485 EUR, two orders above the voltage premium.

A 7-day April week is not 30 days and not two seasons. NR is not OpenDSS. Filling B3/P2/Full requires a calibrated LLM, a DRO solver, trained MAPPO+QP, and FDIA in the loop—the protocol of Section IV, not a result.

VII. Conclusion

A degradation-aware two-stage LP on IEEE 33-bus / SMARD shows that aging belongs in both objectives and that voltage safety is a real-time P–Q job. Table III records that with proven HiGHS euros and leaves B3, P2 and Full blank.
